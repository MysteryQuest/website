## The claim under review

Popular Mechanics reports that a rat anesthesia study is a step toward a quantum theory of consciousness, and suggests that consciousness could be connected with consciousness across the universe. The article's experimental anchor is a 2024 eNeuro paper on microtubule stabilization and isoflurane in rats.

## What the original experiment found

The study measured the time until male rats lost their righting reflex while receiving isoflurane. Animals that had received epothilone B, a microtubule-stabilizing drug, reached that behavioral endpoint later on average than in the control condition. This supports the limited proposition that microtubules may be functionally relevant to this anesthetic response.

## Why the broader inference does not follow

Loss of righting reflex is a conventional and useful animal proxy, not a direct measurement of conscious experience. The experiment did not measure quantum coherence in a brain, demonstrate quantum computation in microtubules, test a human subject, or define and test universe-wide consciousness. Reviews of anesthesia measurement and quantum-consciousness proposals identify those as distinct open problems.

## Evidence workflow

This Lab keeps the source lineage visible: identify the experimental result, record its actual endpoint and limitations, then test each added interpretation against independent sources. A claim can advance only if it has a direct measurement, an operational definition, and independent replication. Until then, it remains a hypothesis or unsupported extension—not a conclusion.
